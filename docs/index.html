The headline, without the technical ledger

What we found

Auerbach’s city-size regularity survives a modern recomputation, but not as a universal constant detached from definitions. His 1913 pattern is compatible with Zipf’s law; the same broad shape remains visible in modern cities even though concentration levels and country ordering have moved. For mountain summits, the pre-registered gentler-than-inverse reading is supported, but no single simple power law describes every arm.

What is new here

Auerbach did not estimate an exponent: his 45–53 band supports only the tolerance ξ ∈ [0.911, 1.089]. His printed 47,8 also proves to be the mean across all 94 cities, not the stabilized tail mean described in his prose. Direct inspection of Appendix Figure A1 in Auerbach and Ciccone (2023) confirms that its reported −1.15 slope is equal-weight OLS of log rank on log population for 94 German cities in 1910 (robust SE 0.03); under this project's notation, its magnitude estimates ζ = 1/ξ, corresponding to ξ approximately 0.87.

1913 cities

The band Auerbach reported begins at rank 15 exactly when his own containment rule is applied. A free-exponent fit over all 94 cities gives ξ = 0.9801, statistically compatible with the Zipf value of 1. The important correction is textual: his printed 47,8 is the mean across all 94 cities, not the stabilized tail mean described in the prose.

Modern cities

Germany still has a Zipf-compatible shape, while its concentration level has shifted. The deliberately coarse Functional-Urban-Area versus municipality contrast is roughly 70% and direction-only, showing that city boundaries are part of the measurement. The positive nine-complex concordance is exploratory, not evidence that the historical ordering is durable.

Mountains

The first qualifiers are bounded support and sampling coverage, before model-family detail: the list-building process pushes estimates toward Auerbach’s direction. Summit heights nevertheless decline more gently with rank in all four primary arms. The result then splits: bounded or cutoff families win in the global, lower-prominence and Himalaya arms, while the Alps, Rockies and highest-prominence tail satisfy the stricter rank-law lane. The evidence supports no tectonic causal mechanism, and the global arm rejects every fitted family on absolute goodness-of-fit.

What the headline does not mean

  • Auerbach supplied a band and descriptive argument, not an estimated exponent.
  • The modern boundary comparison is deliberately coarse and may overstate a suburb-merging effect.
  • Coverage bias in summit lists points toward the mountain result, so that limitation travels with it.

REPORT — Auerbach (1913) Das Gesetz der Bevölkerungskonzentration: the city law and the mountain claim, recomputed

Published: live site · public source · completed 2026-09-03

Abstract

A double-entry transcription of Auerbach's 1913 table reproduces the A.K. band 45–53 and stabilization rank 15, corrects the printed 47,8 as an all-94 rather than tail mean, and yields ξ = 0.9801; the wide 95% interval [0.7787, 1.1851] includes 1 and cannot sharply distinguish nearby exponents. On modern German data, city definition materially changes concentration: the exact FUA-versus-municipality contrast is +72.04%, or roughly 70%, direction-only under this coarse FUA-versus-municipality proxy; the historical-country concordance is exploratory at nine one-to-one complexes and one reassignment away from non-significance. The preregistered mountain test supports a gently declining rank curve but only with its limits adjacent: summit-list coverage bias points toward the result, elevation has bounded support, the global arm rejects every fitted family on absolute fit, and broad cutoff uncertainty leaves the overall claim compatible with qualifiers rather than confirmed.

Notation is the preregistration's §1, used exactly and without mixing: rank-size s(r) = A·r^(-ξ), count law N(s) ∝ s^(-ζ), pdf p(s) ∝ s^(-α), with ζ = 1/ξ and α = ζ + 1. Auerbach's n_x · p_x = const ⇔ ξ = 1 ⇔ ζ = 1 ⇔ α = 2. ξ is estimated everywhere, never assumed.

1. The question

Auerbach (1913, Petermanns Geographische Mitteilungen 59: 74–76, with Tafel 14) proposed a rank law of population concentration. For 94 German places ≥ 50,000 (1910, topographic aggregation) he formed the "Konzentrationszahl" A.K. = rank × population and reported that from rank 15 onward it settles into the band 45–53 with mean 47,8; normalizing by national population gives the "spezifische Konzentration" Sp.K. = A.K. ÷ (population / 10⁸) = 47.8 / 0.645 = 74.1085 → printed 74. He tabulated Sp.K. for twelve complexes (Netherlands 91 down to British India 11), for six Prussian provinces, for Europe as one complex (A.K. 169 over 334 places ≥ 50,000; Sp.K. 39), and a 1895→1910 time series; he noted in passing that the administrative definition gives 77 where the topographic gives 74, a 4.05% definition effect he did not treat as a measurement problem.

Constant A.K. is equivalent to ξ = 1 only at ξ = 1 (prereg §1; Rybski–Ciccone's equivalence is exact there and a hypothesis elsewhere). Auerbach never fitted an exponent: his evidence is a band. Step 0 quantified what the band can carry — 45–53 over ranks 15–94 implies |1 − ξ| ≤ ln(53/45)/ln(94/15) = 0.089159, i.e. ξ ∈ [0.911, 1.089] — so "Zipf confirmed on the 1913 data" is not a statement the band supports beyond that tolerance (prereg F3).

The project's novel target is the paper's closing sentence (AU-C11), for which the dated search found no Auerbach-framed empirical test: "Bei den Gipfelhöhen eines Gebirges ist die Beziehung viel sanfter, der höchste Gipfel eines Gebirges übertrifft die folgenden meist nur wenig." As Step 0 established (prereg F1), the sentence is directionally ambiguous as written, and the ambiguity is a result, not a nuisance:

  • H-MR (primary): the justification clause — the highest summit surpasses the following ones only a little — reads as slow decline of height with rank, ξ < 1.
  • H-MC: on the count-law axis that orders the sentence's own two examples (cities β = 1, wealth β = 2 "schärfer"), "sanfter" reads β < 1 ⇔ ζ < 1 ⇔ ξ > 1.
  • H-MB: the mechanism clause — "die gebirgsbildende Kraft … reichte über eine bestimmte Grenze nicht hinaus" — implies bounded support, so no pure power law; a bounded or truncated family should win the model comparison. On bounded support H-MC is also physically implausible, since ζ < 1 on unbounded support has a divergent mean (receipt D9).

All three are pre-registered and all three are reported (§3.3). Prereg F2 makes bounded support a design constraint, not a footnote: Earth's summit elevations are bounded above by Everest at 8848 m (8848.86 m on the highest-mountains list; the two source articles disagree and neither was reconciled — Stage-3 D15), and every list is bounded below by its own membership rule. Prereg F6 makes the regional arms primary evidence rather than an extension, because Auerbach's claim is framed per range ("eines Gebirges").

The dated novelty sweep (results/stage0-novelty-sweep.md) fixes the delta this project claims. Two papers fit mountain rank–height curves and neither asks Auerbach's question: Miškinis (2011, Geologija 53(1):21–26), on a 548-summit list > 3,500 m, concludes the distribution "is approximated by the exponential and not the power function"; a 2023 J. Applied Mathematics and Physics paper derives a CIR-type gamma tail and fits six regional classifications. Neither cites Auerbach, neither controls for prominence, neither tests an exponent against 1 with calibrated uncertainty, and neither pre-registers a direction. On the city side, the dated sweep found no prior independent-transcription recomputation of Table 1, modern Sp.K. persistence test on his twelve countries, or MLE treatment of his 94 cities — the only exponent estimate on that data found in the sweep is Auerbach and Ciccone's (2023) Appendix Figure A1 (the fourth figure), which reports an equal-weight OLS slope of −1.15 (robust SE 0.03) for log rank on log population. The surviving delta is therefore narrow and specific: Auerbach's own question, on prominence-controlled data, with modern goodness-of-fit and alternative-model testing, and with the directional ambiguity resolved by pre-registration rather than by which reading fits.

3. Results

3.1 The historical anchor (Stage 1)

Table 1 reproduces exactly from the double-entered scan transcription — 94 rows, rank 1 Berlin, ranks consecutive. The printed A.K. over ranks 15–94 has min 45 and max 53 — Auerbach's band, on his own numbers — and the exact products rank × E.Z./100 span 45.12 to 53.10. Under Amendment 1's band-containment criterion the stabilization rank is r₀ = 15, exactly his eyeballed value; the deleted ±2% tail-mean rule had returned r₀ = 92 because it is degenerate as specified, not because it was mis-implemented. Step 0 had quantified a provisional tension by reading the published −1.15 slope with robust SE 0.03 as an estimate of ξ: that hypothetical reading gives a 95% CI of [1.0912, 1.2088], whose lower edge sits just above the band-implied upper bound 1.0892 (receipts D1/D1b). Direct inspection of Appendix Figure A1 establishes the inverse orientation and removes that provisional reading.

The one qualifier is worth stating plainly, because it is the kind of thing a headline loses: the printed 47,8 is an all-94 statistic, not a tail statistic. The mean of the printed column over all 94 ranks is 47.8723 and of the exact products 47.7540 — either truncates or rounds to 47,8 — while the mean over ranks 15–94 is 50.0250 (printed) / 49.8870 (exact). Auerbach's prose frames it as a tail mean ("von der Rangnummer 15 ab … Mittelwert 47,8"). The Tafel-14 annotation is internally inconsistent: its numerator 4503 gives 47.9043, while the sum of the printed column is 4500 → 47.8723, which is what the printed quotient 47,8 is consistent with. Ranks 1–14 fluctuate over 19..46 (Leipzig 19 at rank 3), not the "36–53" the claim inventory first said.

Sp.K. and the surrounding tables confirm: 47.8/0.645 = 74.109 → 74, and the scan (47,8 / 64,5 Mill.) is the arithmetically consistent reading where the translation prints 47,2 / 64,6 (47.2/0.645 = 73.178 → 73, contradicting the printed 74). The twelve-state table verifies cell by cell with one correction — Schweiz A.K. = 2,8, where pass A and Ciccone both print 2,6 — and its implied populations A.K./Sp.K. × 10⁸ match era census figures (Deutsches Reich 64.59, Vereinigte Staaten 92.98, Großbritannien 45.29, Britisch-Indien 327.27 Mill.). The Prussian provinces verify as printed (Rheinland 152, Westfalen 124, Schlesien 88, Hannover 83, Ostpreußen 54, Posen 44 — Posen below Ostpreußen, the one ordering exception Auerbach himself flagged). Europe as one complex verifies: 169/4.32 = 39.1204 → 39 over 334 places. The time-series deltas recompute exactly (density 23.3%, A.K. 72.5%, Sp.K. 40.0% against printed 23/72/40), and the 1910 definition effect is 4.05% on the Sp.K. side and 3.56% on the A.K. side.

The free exponent — the analysis Auerbach never ran. On all 94 printed populations the discrete zeta MLE gives α = 2.0203 → ξ = 0.9801; the wide 95% interval [0.7787, 1.1851] includes 1 and cannot sharply distinguish nearby exponents, while the point estimate sits inside the band-implied window [0.911, 1.089]. Restricting to ranks ≥ 15 requires an upper-truncated zeta (a rank window cuts the top of the distribution; the naive fit inflates α to 2.38) and yields ξ = 1.4383, CI [0.8397, 3.1155] — wide and uninformative at n = 80, with OLS on the same window at 0.9767.

EXT-C1 adjudicated. Direct inspection of Appendix Figure A1 and note 6 in Auerbach and Ciccone (2023) shows log population on the horizontal axis and log rank on the vertical axis and reports an equal-weight ordinary-least-squares slope of −1.15 (robust standard error 0.03; 94 German cities, 1910). The project's separate reproduction of that inverse specification is −1.1489 (HC3 SE 0.0328), whose magnitude the project maps to ζ = 1/ξ and thus ξ = 1/1.1489 = 0.8704 (HC3 CI [0.824, 0.922]); the source does not state the robust covariance convention, use the ξ/ζ notation, or report a population-on-rank OLS coefficient. For comparison, the project's population-on-rank OLS gives ξ = 0.8553 (classical SE 0.0118, HC1 0.0559, HC3 0.0291), and the Gabaix–Ibragimov rank−½ variant gives 0.8027. Source inspection establishes the orientation; the reproduction corroborates it. The mapped ξ ≈ 0.87 sits at or below the band window's lower edge 0.911, and P7 shows why a rank regression should not be treated as a calibrated exponent estimator at this n. The log-log correlation is r = −0.9913 (r² = 0.9827), which is why the plot looks decisive and the estimator is not.

P7, the Monte Carlo. At n = 94 with the fitted zeta as truth (2,000 replicates): the MLE has bias −0.0044, RMSE 0.1007 and nominal-95% coverage 0.943; population-on-rank OLS has bias +0.0588, RMSE 0.1537 and coverage 0.158 classical / 0.636 HC0 / 0.640 HC1 / 0.420 HC3. Severe undercoverage under every SE convention. The source labels its 0.03 only as a robust standard error; the project's HC3 values of 0.0291 and 0.0328 are comparisons, not evidence that the source used HC3. The coverage result is therefore reported per convention rather than attributed to Auerbach and Ciccone.

3.2 Modern cities (Stage 2)

Germany, the Table-1 mirror. On the true 2025 administrative cross-section (n = 131 places ≥ 50,000; national population 83577140) the A.K. band over ranks 15–131 is 57.4 to 87.2, and over all ranks 36.9 to 87.2. The 1910 level (45–53) does not transfer — A.K. scales with city size relative to national population, and a century of urbanization moved it — while exact-count zeta MLE gives α = 1.9261 → ξ = 1.0798, bootstrap 95% CI [0.887, 1.219], with OLS below the MLE at 0.8397 (HC3 0.0157). Compared with the 1913 interval [0.7787, 1.1851], the overlapping intervals do not establish a change in exponent. A.K. mean 75.87 over all ranks and 78.85 over the tail; Sp.K. 90.8, primacy-excluded 92.7.

The definition effect (AU-C9 modern, P4). The topographic arm — 89 Functional Urban Areas, band 71.1 to 158.4, A.K. mean 130.53 — gives Sp.K. 156.2 against the administrative arm's 90.8: +72.04%, or roughly 70%, direction-only under this coarse FUA-versus-municipality proxy, versus Auerbach's own 4.05% in 1910. The proxy is likely to overstate a suburb-merging effect and is not a nested or like-for-like replication. What survives is the direction and the finding that definitional choice materially moves the statistic, which is precisely the mismatch Auerbach disclosed but did not measure.

Persistence of the twelve-country ordering (AU-C5 modern, P3). At the common 100,000 threshold the modern Sp.K. ordering is UK > NL > RU > ES > CH > AT > BE > DE > HU > US > IT > FR > IN, against 1913's NL > UK > BE > CH > DE > US > IT > FR > ES on the nine one-to-one complexes. The ordering does not survive intact (UK/NL swap; Spain 9→3; Belgium 3→5). Kendall τ = +0.5556 on the nine, permutation null (10,000 replicates, primary seed 20260902) mean −0.004, sd 0.265, two-sided p = 0.0436: exploratory at nine one-to-one complexes and one reassignment away from non-significance. The two pre-registered sensitivity arms use a separate permutation stream, seed 20260903: its same-stream primary p is 0.0439, while τ₁ = +0.6364 (p = 0.0058) on eleven complexes (the nine plus the pooled AT+HU successor at Sp.K. 74.8 for Austria-Hungary, plus India as a partial Britisch-Indien successor) and τ₂ = +0.4545 (p = 0.0423) on twelve (τ₁ plus the Russian Federation as the European-Russia successor). The estimate rises when the pooled Austria-Hungary successor joins and falls when the RF joins, illustrating sensitivity to successor assignment rather than durable concordance. All three τ values were re-derived from the derived CSVs for this report (deliver-number-checks.txt C31/C31a–c); the two streams change no inferential decision.

Primacy sensitivity is reported alongside every value, per prereg §4.5, because Sp.K. is primacy-fragile by construction at small n: UK 153.2 → 173.0, AT 94.2 → 63.0 (Vienna's share at n = 6), RU 117.9 → 126.9; India is primacy-insensitive at n = 339 (34.0 → 34.0) once the audit removed the artifact row that had made "Mumbai dominance" look like a 850.9 swing.

The verdict, in the prereg's fixed language. AU-C11's lane is split, and the split is the result:

  • Bounded family wins (H-MB) in A0/A1/A2/A3/R2 — the primary global arm and the prominence sweep and the Himalayas.
  • M-rank supported — the full three-condition confirmation of prereg §5.3 (H-MR significant, power law not rejected on GoF, not beaten by a bounded alternative) — in A4/R1/R3, i.e. the P ≥ 4000 m arm, the Alps and the Rockies.
  • M-count supported: nowhere. H-MC (ξ > 1) is supported in 0/10 arms.
  • E1/E1b carry no lane: they are reported as uninformative, elevation-selected window too narrow (dynamic range 1.23×, parameters on the imposed guards), and their M1-not-rejected results must never be quoted as "the power law fits the highest peaks".

Since prereg F6 makes the regional arms primary evidence, two of the three pre-registered ranges (Alps, Rockies) confirm the preregistered H-MR reading on all three conditions, and the third (Himalayas) sits in the bounded-family lane. Under the §5.3 conjunction, however, AU-C11 overall is not a confirmation: summit-list coverage bias pushes estimates toward the H-MR result, elevation has bounded support, the primary global arm rejects every fitted family on absolute goodness-of-fit, and broad cutoff uncertainty remains under the joint bootstrap. Conditions 2 and 3 fail on that global arm, so in the general §7 vocabulary the result is compatible with qualifiers. The empirical bounded-support implication is compatible with the results; the project tests no tectonic causal mechanism.

H-MR (ξ < 1) is significant in every primary arm. Bootstrap p(ξ ≥ 1) = 0.0000 in all four; LRT p = 3.625×10⁻¹⁰⁴ (A0), 2.168×10⁻¹¹ (R1), 1.236×10⁻³⁶ (R2), 5.42×10⁻¹⁷ (R3); Holm–Bonferroni at family α = 0.05 over the per-arm max of the two frozen statistics gives adjusted p = 1.45e-103 / 2.168e-11 / 3.709e-36 / 1.084e-16 → supported in 4/4. The Holm adjustment was itself an audit correction (F2): the original adjustment ran over all-zero bootstrap p-values and was vacuous. The adjusted values are re-derived from the arm blocks in deliver-number-checks.txt C45a. The prominence sweep and the elevation arms are exploratory and excluded from the family, reported uncorrected.

H-MB and what the winning family looks like. At the selected cutoffs M1 is still rejected wherever the test has power (A0 0.0020, A1 0.0020, A2 0.0359), marginal in A3 0.0838 and R2 0.0519, and not rejected in the small-tail arms A4 0.7665, R1 0.1816, R3 0.8104 (n_tail = 15–40). Bounded alternatives beat M1 on relative Vuong/AICc evidence in A0/A1/A2/A3/R2; in A4/R1/R3 nothing separates from M1 and M1 passes GoF, which is what puts those arms in the M-rank lane. Where the bounded alternatives win, their fitted form is exponential-type rather than a power law with a power-law head: the cutoff-PL's fitted α is negative on A0 (−1.2386, λ = 1256.6 m) and saturated on the regional arms, so the exp(−h/λ) factor does the work. That is Miškinis's "exponential, not power" conclusion re-derived in a different parameterization on prominence-controlled data he did not have, with the arm split retained. The genuinely distinct JAMP-2023 object — a gamma on (0, ∞) with no h_min truncation at all — is reported per arm as a descriptor and excluded from the Vuong/AICc set because its sample differs: on A0 it fits shape b = 5.3292, a = 0.00147529 (scale 677.83 m), mean 3612.3 m, KS 0.1007 over all n = 1522 elevations.

Three honesty notes belong in the body, not a footnote. (i) On A0 all six families are rejected on GoF at the selected cutoff (n_tail = 989, every p = 0.0020), so "bounded family wins" there rests on Vuong/AICc alone (D13). (ii) M4 is unbounded above and per the frozen rule never counts toward H-MB, yet it is the best-AICc model on R2/R3 (and on E1b); its printed GoF p = 1.0000 on those arms is the D11 artifact of 500 of 500 bootstrap replicates failing to refit — its point fits and Vuong statistics are unaffected, but that p-value is not evidence of fit. (iii) M2 and M5 are the same model on [h_min, ∞): h^(−α)exp(−h/λ) ≡ h^(b−1)exp(−ah) at b = 1 − α, a = 1/λ. Their logLik/AICc/KS rows coincide in 8/10 arms and the algebraic identity reproduces from the fitted parameters in 8/8 testable arms; the refitted-bootstrap GoF p still differs between the two rows on R2/R3 because each row draws its own bootstrap, and on E1/E1b the logLik differs slightly because both parameters sit on the imposed guards (α = −60.0, b = 60.0). The model set is therefore five distinct families, and M2/M5 must never be counted as two independent wins.

Auerbach's own wording, tested directly. His justification clause is a statement about the rank curve, and it holds sharply. On A0, h(1)/h(2) = 1.0272 (8848 → 8614 m), the median adjacent relative drop is 0.00068, every adjacent pair is within 5% (share 1.000) and 99.6% are within 1% (0.996). The regional arms are looser but same-signed: R1 1.0380 (share < 1.05: 0.953), R2 1.0305 (0.947), R3 1.0062 (0.943), A1 1.0272 (1.000). All five were recomputed from the derived CSVs for this report and agree with the receipts digit for digit (C53A0–C53R3). Read descriptively, the clause is the best-supported part of AU-C11 — and it is a weaker statement than a power law, which is why it survives while the power-law form does not.

The bias rail (prereg §5.4), with corrected results in hand. The direction was frozen pre-fit: coverage bias pushes ξ̂ down, toward confirming Auerbach. The sweep did exactly that, monotonically: ξ = 0.4598 (P ≥ 1500) → 0.4019 → 0.3853 → 0.3532 → 0.1904 (P ≥ 4000), with wide overlapping CIs throughout. This drift is therefore evidence about the bias, not about the claim. Every H-MR "supported" verdict must be read with the confound stated: the data-collection process pushes in the hypothesis's direction.

Rank-curve OLS versus the distributional MLE. The two disagree in both directions once interior cutoffs are used: ξ_OLS sits below the selected-cutoff ξ_MLE on A0 (0.4015 vs 0.4598), A1–A3 and R1 (0.2487 vs 0.2838), and above it on A4 (0.2632 vs 0.1904), R2 (0.1426 vs 0.1069) and R3 (0.1376 vs 0.1155). The OLS-below-MLE pattern of Stages 1–2 therefore does not carry arm by arm here, and on bounded support the whole-list OLS slope is not a tail-exponent estimate. Both are reported; neither is used for any verdict.

P6, Miškinis. In his own rank space (M6a) the stretched exponential fits R²(log) = 0.99244 on A0, 0.99447 on the Alps, 0.92308 on the Rockies, 0.98891 on E1 — but only 0.81840 on the Himalayas, where the fitted h_max = 7863.2 m falls below the observed 8848 m: his functional form cannot reach Everest in that arm (α_M = 0.4435, RMS 387.0 m). In density form (M6b) it beats M1 on AICc in A0/R1/R2/R3 (−241.15 / −6.47 / −6.85 / −4.44) and ties on E1 (+0.65), and is Vuong-significant on A0 and R2. So "at least as well as any power law" holds; "as well as any alternative" does not — on R2/R3 the unbounded truncated lognormal fits best overall. His qualitative conclusion replicates.

Selection instability is part of the uncertainty, not a defect (D17). The corrected CIs are wide (A0 [0.1164, 0.5218]) and h_min moves under resampling (A0 joint-bootstrap median 2577 m against a selected 2634 m); A0's α CI [2.9165, 9.5924] excludes Auerbach's α = 2 because the entire interval is above it. Metre rounding is immaterial where it matters: ±0.5 m jitter moves ξ by at most 0.0001 across the eight prominence-defined arms and on E1b, and by 0.0020 on the degenerate E1 arm.

AU-C13 stays speculative. The corrected cross-range ordering is R2 0.1069 < R3 0.1155 < R1 0.2838 < A0 0.4598 — Himalayas < Rockies < Alps < global. It is confounded by each arm's elevation span (1.81× to 5.89×), by the coverage bias that pushes every ξ down, and by selection instability (the CIs overlap). 7. Limitations

  1. Coverage bias in every summit list, direction frozen pre-fit. The ultra lists are climbing-community products (peaklist lineage): the Karakoram, Tibet, Alaska, Antarctica and New Guinea entries are the least completely surveyed, the Alps (R1) the best surveyed, the Rockies (R3) intermediate, and the Himalaya arm (R2) mixes well-surveyed Nepal with sparsely surveyed Sino-Nepal provinces — the arm where selection jumps to 6495 m, consistent with a two-regime list. Missed remote summits are mid-height, so undercoverage and climbed-peak overrepresentation both bias ξ̂ downward, i.e. toward Auerbach. The prominence sweep moved monotonically in exactly that predicted direction, so the sweep is evidence about the bias and cannot be read as evidence about the law.
  2. Cutoff selection dominates the mountain uncertainty. The corrected CIs are wide (A0 [0.1164, 0.5218]) and h_min moves under resampling (A0 median 2577 m vs selected 2634 m). Conditional statements understate this; the compatibility readings here are on the joint-bootstrap intervals. M2–M6 CIs are bootstrapped with h_min fixed (B = 500) against M1's joint bootstrap (B = 2000 on the primary arms), so their intervals do not carry selection uncertainty at all (D4).
  3. A list's membership rule is a lower cutoff, and elevation support is bounded above. Every arm is truncated below by its own prominence threshold and above by Earth's maximum; the pure full-support power law is false a priori (prereg F2), which is why it is reported as the comparator it is rather than as a candidate truth. The two source articles disagree on individual elevations (Everest 8848 vs 8848.86 m; K2 8614 vs 8611 m) and neither was reconciled — each arm keeps its own source's values.
  4. Binned, defined and vintaged city data. Auerbach's A.K. is hand-rounded (round-to-nearest on 80/94 rows, 11 rows off by ≥ 0.5, max 1.08); modern arms mix reference years (IN 2011 … DE/IT/CH 2025) so the τ is not a same-year comparison; Sp.K. levels are not comparable across 1910 and 2025 because urbanization raises A.K. relative to national population, so only orderings, ratios and ξ are compared across time; and the topographic arm exists for Germany only.
  5. Sp.K. is a self-normalized statistic and primacy-fragile. Its denominator assumes largest-city scale ∝ national population, which fails for small countries and primate systems; at n = 6 (Austria) excluding the primate moves Sp.K. from 94.2 to 63.0. Every modern table therefore carries its primacy-excluded counterpart, per prereg §4.5.

  9. What this report is not. No mechanistic claim about tectonic "driving forces" (AU-C13 stays speculative); no wealth-distribution test (AU-C12 parked); no bibliometric limb (the Rybski–Ciccone ALZ claims belong to a different paper and a separate future project).
