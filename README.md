# Auerbach (1913) *Das Gesetz der Bevölkerungskonzentration* — the city law and the mountain claim, recomputed

An executable verification of the paper that preceded Zipf's law for cities, on its own 1913 tables and on modern data — plus the first Auerbach-framed statistical test of the mountain claim he closed the article with. **Verdict, one sentence:** *Auerbach's city band and stabilization rank reproduce exactly from a double-entry transcription of the scan, while a free-exponent MLE gives ξ = 0.9801 and the wide 95% interval [0.7787, 1.1851] includes 1 and cannot sharply distinguish nearby exponents; on modern data concentration levels and country ordering move, but overlapping historical and modern exponent intervals do not establish a change in exponent; and his mountain sentence — directionally ambiguous as written — supports the preregistered ξ < 1 reading only with coverage, bounded-support, absolute-fit, and cutoff qualifiers, so the claim lands as "compatible with qualifiers", not "confirmed".*

**Project status:** complete. The published analysis, audit chain, source custody, and retired-before-fit Scaruffi disposition are closed; the possibilities noted later are out of scope, not open project work.

Felix Auerbach's 1913 article in *Petermanns Geographische Mitteilungen* formed, for 94 German places ≥ 50,000 (1910 census, topographic aggregation), the "Konzentrationszahl" **A.K. = rank × population**, and reported that from rank 15 onward it settles into the band **45–53** with mean **47,8**; normalised by national population this gives the "spezifische Konzentration" **Sp.K. = 74**. He tabulated Sp.K. for twelve complexes (Netherlands 91 down to British India 11), for six Prussian provinces, for Europe as one complex, and a 1895→1910 time series; he noted in passing that the administrative definition gives 77 where the topographic gives 74 — a definition effect he disclosed but never measured. Then, in the closing section, he wrote that for **the summits of a mountain range** the rank relation is "viel sanfter", the highest summit surpassing the following ones only a little, because the mountain-building force "did not reach beyond a certain limit".

Constant A.K. is equivalent to ξ = 1 *only at* ξ = 1. **Auerbach never fitted an exponent** — his evidence is a band. Step 0 of this project quantified what that band can carry: 45–53 over ranks 15–94 implies ξ ∈ [0.911, 1.089], so "Zipf confirmed on the 1913 data" is not a statement the band supports beyond that tolerance. Everything below is estimated under a pre-registration frozen on 2026-09-01, **before any exponent was fitted**, with the notation used exactly and never mixed:

> rank-size `s(r) = A·r^(−ξ)` · count law `N(s) ∝ s^(−ζ)` · pdf `p(s) ∝ s^(−α)` · **ζ = 1/ξ**, **α = ζ + 1** · Auerbach's law ⇔ **ξ = 1** ⇔ ζ = 1 ⇔ α = 2

**Site:** a short overview, the full report and the interactive explorer are hosted at https://kenrinzero.github.io/auerbach-cities-and-mountains/ — a self-contained `docs/index.html` (no network, no external assets, no dependencies) regenerated from `REPORT.md` and the frozen receipts by `src/build_explorer.py`. The Overview tab is the reader-facing entry point; the Full report tab is rendered verbatim from `REPORT.md`, so the technical record cannot drift from the text the numeral checks govern.

The defensible claim, the adjudication of all thirteen of Auerbach's claims plus three external numbers, and nine explicit limitations are in **[`REPORT.md`](REPORT.md)**.

## Headline findings

1. **The historical anchor reproduces, with one qualifier that a headline would lose.** Table 1 reproduces *exactly* — 94 rows, Berlin rank 1, printed A.K. min 45 / max 53 over ranks 15–94. Under Auerbach's own band-containment criterion the stabilization rank is **r₀ = 15**, precisely his eyeballed value. But the printed **47,8 is an all-94 mean, not the tail mean his prose states**: the mean over all 94 ranks is 47.8723 (printed column) / 47.7540 (exact products), while the mean over ranks 15–94 is 50.0250 / 49.8870. Sp.K., the twelve-state table, the provinces, the Europe complex and all three time-series deltas verify; one cell is corrected against both prior readings (**Schweiz A.K. = 2,8**, not 2,6).
2. **The free exponent he never ran: ξ = 0.9801.** Discrete zeta MLE on all 94 printed integer populations gives α = 2.0203 → **ξ = 0.9801**; the wide 95% interval [0.7787, 1.1851] includes 1 and cannot sharply distinguish nearby exponents, while the point estimate lies inside the band-implied window [0.911, 1.089].
3. **The published −1.15 slope is an inverse-axis estimate of ζ, not ξ.** Direct inspection of Appendix Figure A1 and note 6 in Auerbach and Ciccone (2023) shows log population on the horizontal axis and log rank on the vertical axis and reports an equal-weight ordinary-least-squares slope of **−1.15** (robust standard error 0.03; 94 German cities, 1910). The project's separate reproduction of that inverse specification is **−1.1489** (HC3 SE 0.0328), whose magnitude the project maps to ζ = 1/ξ and thus **ξ = 1/1.1489 = 0.8704**; the source does not state the robust covariance convention, use the ξ/ζ notation, or report a population-on-rank OLS coefficient.
4. **Rank-regression is not a calibrated instrument at n = 94, and the MLE is.** Monte Carlo at the real n, with the fitted zeta as truth: the MLE has bias −0.0044, RMSE 0.1007 and nominal-95% coverage **0.943**; rank-size OLS has bias +0.0588 and coverage **0.158 classical / 0.636 HC0 / 0.640 HC1 / 0.420 HC3** — severe undercoverage under every standard-error convention. The log-log correlation is r = −0.9913, which is exactly why the plot looks decisive and the estimator is not.
5. **Modern Germany: the level moves; the exponent comparison is unresolved.** On the true 2025 administrative cross-section (n = 131 places ≥ 50,000) exact-count MLE gives **ξ = 1.0798**, CI [0.887, 1.219], while the A.K. band has displaced to 57.4–87.2. Compared with the 1913 CI [0.7787, 1.1851], the overlapping intervals do not establish a change in exponent.
6. **The definition effect is far larger than Auerbach's 4.05%.** Functional Urban Areas (the modern stand-in for his suburb-merging) give Sp.K. **156.2** against the administrative arm's **90.8** — **+72.04%**, or roughly 70%, direction-only under this coarse FUA-versus-municipality proxy. It is likely to *overstate* a suburb-merging effect and is not a like-for-like replication. What survives is that definitional choice materially moves the statistic — the mismatch Auerbach disclosed and never measured.
7. **The twelve-country ordering does not survive 110 years intact; the residual association is exploratory.** UK/NL swap, Spain 9→3, Belgium 3→5. Kendall **τ = +0.5556**, permutation p = **0.0436** (10,000 replicates, seed 20260902), is exploratory at nine one-to-one complexes and one reassignment away from non-significance, with the two preregistered successor arms at **τ₁ = +0.6364 (p = 0.0058)** and **τ₂ = +0.4545 (p = 0.0423)** on a separate permutation stream.
8. **The mountain claim: directionally ambiguous, preregistered, and split by arm.** Step 0 established that the sentence reads ξ < 1 on one axis and ξ > 1 on another, so all three readings were frozen as hypotheses before fitting. **ξ < 1 in all ten arms**; H-MR (ξ < 1) is significant in all four primary arms after Holm–Bonferroni (largest adjusted p = 2.17×10⁻¹¹, the Alps). But summit-list coverage bias pushes estimates toward that result, elevation has bounded support, and broad cutoff uncertainty remains. Relative evidence gives **bounded family wins (H-MB)** on the global arm and the prominence sweep and the Himalayas, while **M-rank is fully supported** at P ≥ 4000 m, in the Alps and in the Rockies; **M-count is supported nowhere**. A4 is the companion-evidence edge case: M1 passes GoF at p = 0.7665 and no bounded alternative wins on the preregistered significant comparison. AICc is companion evidence, not part of that alternative-win rule: the project-side M6b fit has lower companion AICc (ΔAICc −25.47), but its Vuong p = 0.5619 is not significant and it fails absolute GoF at p = 0.0020. On the global arm all six fitted families are rejected on absolute goodness-of-fit (p = 0.0020), so "bounded family wins" there rests on Vuong/AICc alone.
9. **Auerbach's own justification clause holds sharply — and it is a weaker statement than a power law, which is why it survives.** On the global arm h(1)/h(2) = 1.0272 (8848 → 8614 m); *every* adjacent pair is within 5% and 99.6% within 1%.
10. **Miškinis's "exponential, not power" conclusion replicates on data he did not have.** Where bounded alternatives win, their fitted form is exponential-type rather than a power law with a power-law head: the cutoff-power-law's fitted α is *negative* on the global arm (−1.2386, λ = 1256.6 m), so the exp(−h/λ) factor does the work.
11. **The bias rail fired exactly as pre-registered, and it is a limitation, not a result.** Coverage bias in prominence lists was frozen pre-fit as pushing ξ̂ *downward* — toward confirming Auerbach. The prominence sweep did precisely that, monotonically (0.4598 → 0.4019 → 0.3853 → 0.3532 → 0.1904). That drift is evidence about the bias, not about the claim, and every H-MR verdict travels with the confound stated.

## Reproduce

Tested with **Python 3.13.13, numpy 2.5.2, scipy 1.18.1** on Windows. ~8 minutes end to end, dominated by the Stage-3 fit.

```bash
python src/step0_derivation_checks.py   # -> results/step0-derivation-checks.txt   (26 derivation receipts)
python src/stage1_recompute.py          # -> results/stage1-recompute.txt          (~2 min)
python src/stage2_parse_raw.py          # -> data/derived/modern-*.csv             (raw -> derived, contract rule 2)
python src/stage2_modern.py             # -> results/stage2-recompute.txt

python src/stage3_mountains.py          # -> results/stage3-recompute.txt          (~5 min; the fit receipts)

python src/build_explorer.py            # -> results/explorer.html AND docs/index.html (asserted byte-identical)
python -m unittest discover -s tests -q # current regression suite
```

**This was run, not merely written.** The complete analytical pipeline above was executed in an isolated copy of this repository, from the raw data in `data/raw/` alone, and **every protected analytical artifact came back byte-identical** — including `results/stage3-recompute.txt` at SHA-256 `6ee0540c11ab60ef…` (the receipt `REPORT.md` §8 quotes) and `results/deliver-number-checks.txt` at `0c160505b685ced6…`. The then-current explorer also reproduced at `294074ad29d1adcd…`; later reader-facing builds and their separate deterministic checks are recorded in the dated audit chain. The derived tables are additionally hash-manifested at `data/derived/MANIFEST.sha256`, so a reader can verify a regeneration rather than trust it. `src/stage3_mountains.py` emits one benign `RuntimeWarning` from a scipy simplex convergence test on a saturated parameter; it does not affect any fitted value.

Nothing in the pipeline reaches the network. The explorer is asserted self-contained at build time — it fails the build if any `<script src>`, `<link>` other than the exact self-contained `<link rel="icon" href="data:,">`, `@import`, `url(http…)`, `<img>`, `<iframe>`, `fetch(` or `XMLHttpRequest` appears — and opens from `file://` with the network disabled.

### Prior art on mountain rank–height distributions

The dated novelty sweep is the authority for what this project claims as new; these are the papers it found.

- **Miškinis, P. (2011).** "Mathematical modelling of mountain height distribution on the Earth's surface." *Geologija*, **53**(1(73)), 21–26. https://doi.org/10.6001/geologija.v53i1.1615 — Paulius Miškinis, Vilnius Gediminas Technical University. Fits a 548-summit list (> 3,500 m) and concludes the distribution "is approximated by the exponential and not the power function". His stretched-exponential rank curve `h(i) = h_max·exp(−β(i−1)^(1/α))` enters our model set as a first-class alternative (M6a natively, M6b as a renormalised bounded density), and his qualitative conclusion **replicates** on prominence-controlled data he did not have — with one arm where his functional form cannot reach Everest (Himalayas: fitted h_max = 7863 m against an observed 8848 m, R²(log) = 0.818 where the other arms exceed 0.92).
- **Allen, E. J. (2023).** "Derivation of a Formula for Mountain Height as a Function of Rank in Height." *Journal of Applied Mathematics and Physics*, **11**(11), 3565–3584. https://doi.org/10.4236/jamp.2023.1111225 — Scientific Research Publishing. Derives a CIR-type SDE for elevation dynamics giving a gamma-like tail `p(h) ∝ h^(b−1)·exp(−a·h)`, fitted to six regional classifications. Used here as prior art on **model families only** (our M5), not as authority: the venue is SCIRP, and our novelty sweep records that judgement explicitly. Its genuinely distinct object — a gamma on (0, ∞) with no lower truncation at all — is reported per arm as a descriptor and **excluded** from the Vuong/AICc comparison because its sample differs.
- **Scaruffi, P. (2008).** *Highest mountains in the world* (compiled list, ≥ 3,500 m). http://www.scaruffi.com/travel/tallest.html — Miškinis's stated 548-summit source. **Status correction and closure:** the Stage-3 path probe returned 404, but the page was later obtained and preserved. ### Citation-variance notes

Two places where sources disagree, recorded so a reader who checks does not think we missed it.

1. **Auerbach (1913) volume/issue/pages.** We cite **59**, 74–76. The held scan shows the article beginning on printed page 74, ending on page 76, followed by Tafel 14, but does not establish an issue designation. Secondary records disagree: the SAGE page and translation use “59(I):74–76”, Rybski (2013) prints “59, 73–76”, and Rybski & Ciccone (2023) print the internally inconsistent “59 (74): 74–76”. Because no primary support for “(I)” was found, the issue is omitted rather than guessed; the earlier “51–55” is unsupported and remains superseded.
2. **Saibante's α convention.** His α is not our α. Reading his table as a pdf exponent would invert every value; the flip is asserted in code, not assumed (receipt D6).
